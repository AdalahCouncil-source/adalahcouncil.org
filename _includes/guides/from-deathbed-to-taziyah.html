1Before death

Prompting the shahādah is the one practice here with a sound, explicit text. Facing the qiblah is agreed by the schools on weaker evidence, and reciting Yā-Sīn rests on a weak report.

PracticeBasisSchool notes
Hope in Allah’s mercy“Let none of you die except thinking well of Allah” (Muslim)Agreed. Visitors speak of mercy, not fear.
Talqīn: lā ilāha illā Allāh“Prompt your dying with lā ilāha illā Allāh” (Muslim)Agreed. Said within hearing, not commanded. Once they say it, it is not repeated unless they speak of something else.

End-of-life medical decisions, such as life support, DNR orders and organ donation, need their own fatwa and are outside this guide.

2At the moment of death

The immediate sunnah is short: close the eyes, say good words, cover the body, and hasten everything that follows.

  1. Close the eyes and pray for them. The Prophet ﷺ closed Abū Salamah’s eyes, said the sight follows the soul, and told the family to say only good, since the angels say āmīn (Muslim).
  2. Say the istirjāʿ: innā lillāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn, Allāhumma ʾjurnī fī muṣībatī wa-akhlif lī khayran minhā (Muslim).
  3. Bind the jaw, ease the joints and straighten the limbs while the body is still soft.
  4. Remove the clothing and cover the whole body with a sheet, as the Prophet ﷺ was covered with a striped cloth (Bukhārī, Muslim).Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī: a light weight on the abdomen.
  5. Kissing the deceased is permitted. Abū Bakr kissed the Prophet ﷺ after his death (Bukhārī).
  6. Weeping is permitted; wailing is not. Wailing, striking the face and tearing clothes are prohibited (Bukhārī, Muslim).
  7. Hasten. “Hurry with the bier” (Bukhārī, Muslim). Delay only for identification, legal requirements, or close family arriving soon.
  8. Settle debts promptly from the estate.
  9. Announce the death so people pray and attend; the Prophet ﷺ announced al-Najāshī’s death (Bukhārī). The boastful pre-Islamic proclamation is what is disliked.

In Virginia, confirm with the funeral home: a death certificate and burial permit are needed before burial; ask that the body not be embalmed; a medical examiner case may delay release.

Same sex washes same sex in all four schools. The differences are over spouses, the age of a child, ranking, and what to do when no permitted washer is present.

4The washing

The obligatory minimum in every school is one wash reaching the whole body after visible impurity is removed. Everything below is sunnah.

  1. Prepare a raised bed, inclined so water runs toward the feet. Privacy; only needed helpers present.
  2. Cover navel to knee. Touching or looking at the ʿawrah is prohibited; a cloth or glove on the hand throughout.Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Ḥanbalī: remove the clothing. Shāfiʿī: preferably wash under a thin shirt, as the Prophet ﷺ was washed.
  3. Intention and basmalah.Ḥanbalī: the washer’s intention is a condition. Shāfiʿī: not a condition. Ḥanafī: not needed for validity, but needed for the communal duty to be discharged.
  4. Press the abdomen gently so remnants exit. Never for a pregnant woman.Ḥanbalī, Shāfiʿī: raise the upper body and press before istinjāʾ. Ḥanafī: wipe the abdomen gently during the wash.
  5. Istinjāʾ with a covered hand; change the covering after.
  6. Wuḍūʾ of the deceased, beginning on the right (Bukhārī, Muslim).Ḥanafī: no rinsing of mouth or nose. Ḥanbalī: wipe teeth and nostrils with a wet cloth. Shāfiʿī: rinse with the head inclined so water does not go down.
  7. Head and beard with sidr or soap. Sidr is Ziziphus (Christ’s thorn, jujube), not lotus.
  8. Body: right before left, upper before lower.Ḥanafī method: lay on the left side to wash the right, then on the right side to wash the left.
  9. An odd number of washes: three, then five or seven as needed (Umm ʿAṭiyyah, Bukhārī and Muslim).Ḥanbalī: beyond seven if still unclean, ending on an odd count.
  10. Camphor in the final wash.
  11. Nails and moustache.Ḥanbalī: may be trimmed if long; clippings go in the shroud. Ḥanafī, Mālikī: not cut. Shāfiʿī: two opinions; al-Nawawī preferred not to cut.
  12. A woman’s hair.Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī: three braids behind her (Bukhārī). Ḥanafī: two plaits laid on the chest over the shirt at shrouding.
  13. Dry with a clean cloth, keep the ʿawrah covered, and move to shrouding.

The muḥrim hadith: a man thrown by his camel; the Prophet ﷺ said to wash him with water and sidr, shroud him in his two garments, and not cover his head, for he will be raised saying the talbiyah (Bukhārī, Muslim). Muslim’s wording forbids covering the face as well. Shāfiʿīs and Ḥanbalīs take it as general law; Ḥanafīs and Mālikīs hold it particular to that man.

7Taʿziyah and mourning

Taʿziyah is sunnah in all four schools. They differ on formal sittings to receive mourners, the three-day limit, and whether recited Qurʾān reaches the deceased.

The Prophet’s ﷺ words to his grieving daughter: inna lillāhi mā akhadha wa-lahu mā aʿṭā, wa-kullu shayʾin ʿindahu bi-ajalin musammā, fal-taṣbir wal-taḥtasib (Bukhārī, Muslim). Any words of patience and prayer suffice.

8Cultural practices

Many families bring funeral and mourning customs from their home cultures. A custom that contradicts no text is permitted as custom. It becomes a problem when it is claimed as sunnah, treated as required, or allowed to delay burial or burden the bereaved. Nothing in this section should be taught as sunnah, and no family should be faulted for leaving any of it out.

Prohibited whatever the culture

  • Wailing, hired mourners, striking the face, tearing clothes.
  • The bereaved family paying for a feast for guests.
  • Delaying burial for display, the travel of distant relatives, or ceremony.
  • Writing Qurʾān or the shahādah on the kafan, where it will be soiled.
  • Placing parchments, cards or cloths bearing Qurʾān, the shahādah or the names of Allah in the kafan or the grave, including the ʿahdnāmah. Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ forbade writing Yā-Sīn or al-Kahf on the kafan for fear of the body’s fluids, and Ibn Ḥajar al-Haytamī rejected the minority view permitting the ʿahdnāmah. Some Ḥanafī texts relay permission (al-Ṣaffār, cited in al-Durr al-Mukhtār), but Ibn ʿĀbidīn concludes that the prohibition applies all the more here, absent a sound hadith. The ʿahd duʿāʾ itself is authentic only as Ibn Masʿūd’s own teaching, a duʿāʾ for the living to say; nothing in it asks for it to be written. Pray for the deceased instead of burying the words with them.
  • Treating any custom above as obligatory, or blaming a family who omits it.
